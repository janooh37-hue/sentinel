# Research: manageable department and organization setup

Research date: 2026-10-24. Scope: public, primary-source product documentation, vendor training and vendor-authored guidance. This is a workflow comparison, not a hands-on usability test or a claim that any product's interface has been proven best for this organization.

## Decision in brief

**Recommendation [INFERENCE]: make a searchable department register the main administration screen, with a short “choose a head, check the people, configure access and services” flow. Keep the reporting chart available for understanding relationships, but do not require navigating or drawing it to create a department.** This combines the department register/form pattern documented by [Odoo](https://www.odoo.com/documentation/18.0/applications/hr/employees/departments.html), the list/import maintenance paths documented by [Personio](https://support.personio.de/hc/en-us/articles/18862110441885-Set-up-departments-and-teams), and the separation of scope from permissions documented by [Rippling](https://www.rippling.com/platform/permissions). It also follows [NN/g's progressive-disclosure guidance](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/progressive-disclosure/) and [PatternFly's warning against deep trees containing hundreds of items](https://www.patternfly.org/components/tree-view/design-guidelines/).

### Case inputs and boundaries

The assignment supplies these case facts, not newly measured research findings: 304 employees; 8 duty units; 296 supervisor links; one root per unit; up to 8 reporting levels; 174 people who supervise someone; about 10 user accounts; an administrative-office unit of 69 people; Arabic and English administration. Eight roots without supervisors are expected in this supplied forest. Do not label them as eight broken links merely by subtracting 296 from 304.

The approved local model is authoritative: a department is an admin-selected head and the branch below them; sub-departments are derived, supervisors have their own branch access, and deputies, viewers, exceptions and services remain explicit controls. Reporting-link editing remains governed by `employees.edit`, not a new department-only restriction. See the [approved model and scope rules](department-orgtree-rework.md#user-decisions-verbatim-intent), [derivation](department-orgtree-rework.md#derivation-backendappservicesdepartment_tree_servicepy-new), and [access rules](department-orgtree-rework.md#scope-department_scope_service). The intended experience must fit short, formal, auditable HR interactions and equal Arabic/English use, not an elaborate enterprise modeling exercise: [PRODUCT.md](../../PRODUCT.md#users), [product principles](../../PRODUCT.md#product-principles), and [DESIGN.md](../../DESIGN.md).

### Evidence conventions and limitations

- **Documented** means stated in the linked vendor-owned documentation or training. A vendor product page establishes an advertised capability, not exact interaction behavior. Staff community guidance is identified as such. Recommendations and comparisons are labeled **[INFERENCE]**.
- BambooHR's detailed help articles returned a loading/login shell to the researcher; some findings below are explicitly limited to indexed excerpts. HiBob's core help center requires customer login. Rippling's help center did not expose readable article text. SAP's `help.sap.com` pages exposed a JavaScript shell, so the SAP findings below rely on readable first-party `learning.sap.com` lessons instead. These are research-access limits, not evidence that a feature is absent.
- Where no first-run screen, inline editor, wizard, exception rule or specific access behavior was documented in the sources read, that is recorded as **not established**, rather than “unsupported.” Employee onboarding is not evidence of an administrator's organization-setup flow. Editions and tenant rollouts can differ; this document is not a licensing comparison.
- No external source is asserted to validate Sentinel's exact combination of membership and access semantics. In particular, Workday supervisory organizations, Google organizational units and Entra administrative units are not interchangeable with Sentinel departments; the distinctions below matter.

## 1. Vendor findings

### 1.1 BambooHR

**Definition.** `department` is an employee list field, described as the employee's current department unless reading Job History. The reporting relationship is separately represented by `reportsTo`. This establishes separate department and reporting data, not a rule that a department consists of a manager's descendants. A department-head object or automatic department-parent hierarchy was not established in the sources read. [BambooHR field reference](https://documentation.bamboohr.com/docs/list-of-field-names)

**Setup and maintenance.** BambooHR documents **Power Edit** for updating multiple employee profiles and company-field settings for fields such as department. Its first-party reseller training documents separate import tools for employee fields and table/history data, warns that imports have no undo, and recommends a test with one employee before mass import. This is evidence for bulk/profile maintenance, not a chart-drawing setup workflow. [Customization guide](https://www.bamboohr.com/resources/guides/100-ways-to-customize-bamboohr), [Import Tool training](https://partners.bamboohr.com/reseller-training-resources/import-tool-training)

**Org chart.** The indexed excerpt for “View the Company Directory and Org Chart” places it under **People → Org Chart**, initially showing top officials and their direct reports. The excerpt says it is generated from manager assignments and is changed by updating **Reports To** in employee profiles. The full help page was not readable, so a broader claim that no chart-editing shortcut exists is not justified. [Official help article, indexed excerpt](https://help.bamboohr.com/s/article/587751)

**Access.** The Manager access level can view information about direct and indirect reports, with configurable hidden fields; the vendor's explanation says its editing ability is limited to notes. Employee access is separately configurable for the employee's own information; custom levels provide additional administrative or editing permissions. Department-filter details for custom levels were not established from readable first-party help. Crucially, directory/org-chart sharing is not governed by per-employee record permissions: seeing a directory is not equivalent to seeing sensitive HR records. [Access-level explanation](https://www.bamboohr.com/blog/access-levels-bamboohr), [Directory API access semantics](https://documentation.bamboohr.com/reference/get-employees-directory)

**Exceptions.** Custom access levels are a documented mechanism for differing permissions, but a separate per-person exception list on a department-derived scope was not established. Do not assume that a custom level is equivalent to Sentinel's membership override. [Access levels](https://www.bamboohr.com/blog/access-levels-bamboohr)

**First-run guidance.** The vendor's QuickStart training covers enabling employee and manager access and previewing the system as another user. The import training supplies practical risk guidance. A department-specific empty-state screen or first-run wizard was not observed. [Enable Employee Access course](https://learn.bamboohr.com/enable-employee-access), [Import training](https://partners.bamboohr.com/reseller-training-resources/import-tool-training)

**Transferable lesson [INFERENCE].** Maintain reporting data through identifiable employee records and bulk tools; make ordinary organization viewing distinct from sensitive-data authorization. The cited sources do not support recreating BambooHR as a department canvas editor.

### 1.2 HiBob / Bob

**Definition.** Department is `work.department`, a list-valued work field; reporting manager is `work.reportsTo`. The API documents hierarchy lists with a `parentId`, but the sources read do not establish that the standard Department field is such a list or that its membership derives from reporting lines. [Reading employee data](https://apidocs.hibob.com/docs/how-to-read-employee-data), [Working with lists](https://apidocs.hibob.com/docs/how-to-work-with-lists-public-api), [Adding a list item](https://apidocs.hibob.com/reference/post_company-named-lists-listname)

**Setup and maintenance.** Bob's own Training Village teaches creating and updating people data in bulk with **Import**, including table-data imports. Its API documentation distinguishes employee fields from historical work-table entries with effective dates. These support a data-first setup/maintenance model; they do not establish an in-product department wizard or inline grid editor. [People data imports course](https://training.hibob.com/people-data-imports), [Updating employee data](https://apidocs.hibob.com/docs/update-employee-data)

**Org chart.** HiBob advertises a chart of people, teams and departments with configurable layers of data. The exact core-chart editing/reparenting interaction could not be established because the detailed help center was gated. Treat it as a documented visualization capability with **editor status unverified**, not as a proven read-only chart or drag editor. [Core HR feature page](https://www.hibob.com/features/core-hr-b/)

**Access.** A HiBob staff-authored community recipe distinguishes **Group Members** from **People's data → Access data for**. It creates a group with `Is Manager equals Yes`, then limits the people whose data are visible using `Reports to is Viewer`. The same recipe explicitly removes categories from the default Managers group to remove direct-and-indirect-report access before granting direct-only access. This is concrete evidence of manager-chain access plus separate recipient and target-population configuration. It is staff guidance, not the gated product manual. [How to Limit Managers' Access to Their Direct Reports Only](https://heartcorehr.hibob.com/permission-groups-186/how-to-limit-managers-access-to-their-direct-reports-only-6650), [API service-user permission setup](https://apidocs.hibob.com/docs/api-service-users)

**Exceptions.** A custom permission group can change which managers receive a permission and which people it covers, as the staff recipe demonstrates. A native “move this employee to a different derived department” exception, and the exact department-plus-sub-department permission rule, were not established. The recipe also warns that some manager features, such as 1:1s, remain specific to the default Managers group, so replacing a default group can have side effects. [Staff permission recipe](https://heartcorehr.hibob.com/permission-groups-186/how-to-limit-managers-access-to-their-direct-reports-only-6650)

**First-run guidance.** The public training catalog offers people-data-import training within broader onboarding tracks. A first-run department empty state was not observed, and the catalog is not evidence of an in-product setup wizard. [Training Village](https://training.hibob.com/people-data-imports)

**Transferable lesson [INFERENCE].** Explain “who receives access” separately from “whose data they receive.” Do not make the non-expert maintain many overlapping permission groups merely to choose department heads.

### 1.3 Personio

**Definition.** Personio has a department/sub-department hierarchy and a separate optional team/sub-team structure. An employee is assigned to a sub-department; parent departments are assigned automatically. Reporting lines instead use the **Supervisor** attribute. This is an explicit department tree alongside a reporting tree, not Sentinel's manager-anchored membership. [Set up departments and teams](https://support.personio.de/hc/en-us/articles/18862110441885-Set-up-departments-and-teams), [Manage the Org chart](https://support.personio.de/hc/en-us/articles/29762795544989-Manage-the-Org-chart)

**Setup and maintenance.** The documented sequence is **Settings → Organization → Departments and teams → Add new → choose parent → assign employees through profiles, People list or import**. Department/team definitions can be imported separately using Name, Parent and Updated name columns. The People list supports filtering a department, selecting its employees, and bulk editing an attribute and effective date. This gives occasional setup and recurring bulk maintenance separate paths. [Setup guide](https://support.personio.de/hc/en-us/articles/18862110441885-Set-up-departments-and-teams), [Import departments and teams](https://support.personio.de/hc/en-us/articles/25768407987741-Import-departments-and-teams), [People list](https://support.personio.de/hc/en-us/articles/115001324149-Overview-of-the-People-List)

**Org chart.** People view uses Supervisor, while Departments and Teams views use their corresponding attributes. Admins can assign up to three leads by opening a department/team card and editing it. **Those leads are display-only and do not affect other parts of Personio; they cannot be imported.** Therefore, “Personio lets you choose a department lead in a chart” must not be cited as evidence that doing so grants department access. Placeholder cards preserve reporting-chain context when the viewer cannot see an intermediate person. [Manage the Org chart](https://support.personio.de/hc/en-us/articles/29762795544989-Manage-the-Org-chart)

**Access.** Personio makes department scope depth explicit: **assigned department**, **assigned and sub-departments**, or **entire department hierarchy**. Reporting-line permissions are configured separately through Relationships/Supervisor. Employee filters offer custom target populations, and the documentation notes that My reports, Custom and All do not automatically include the viewer's own data; Self is separate. [Department scope choices](https://support.personio.de/hc/en-us/articles/18862110441885-Set-up-departments-and-teams), [Configure reporting-line access](https://support.personio.de/hc/en-us/articles/360000073569-Configure-reporting-line-access), [Custom employee filters](https://support.personio.de/hc/en-us/articles/360000323158-Use-employee-filters-to-grant-your-employees-custom-permissions)

**Exceptions.** The People list can add or remove people from roles, giving an explicit role-assignment maintenance path. The reviewed help does not establish a Sentinel-style per-person override of derived department membership. Do not equate assignment to another permission role with relocating an employee in an organization. [People list role actions](https://support.personio.de/hc/en-us/articles/115001324149-Overview-of-the-People-List)

**First-run guidance.** The setup guide first asks the admin to prepare an overview of the organizational structure, illustrates traditional versus matrix structures, requires Departments, and says to add Teams only when a matrix structure needs them. It distinguishes never-configured accounts from existing accounts migrating to hierarchical departments. That is concrete setup guidance; an actual first-run empty-state interaction was not observed. [Setup and migration guidance](https://support.personio.de/hc/en-us/articles/18862110441885-Set-up-departments-and-teams)

**Transferable lesson [INFERENCE].** Provide a department list and clear scope language. Borrow explicit “this department” versus “including sub-departments” explanations, but preserve Sentinel's existing role semantics instead of importing Personio's display-only lead model.

### 1.4 Rippling

**Definition.** Departments are separate hierarchical records with `name`, `parent_id`, and an ordered department-hierarchy path. The vendor explicitly connects an employee's assigned department to app assignment, administrative access, documents and onboarding. A department-head field that automatically establishes branch membership was not established. [Department data guide](https://developer.rippling.com/documentation/rest-api/guides/departments)

**Setup and maintenance.** The API documents department list/create/update operations, with a required name and optional parent. Rippling's HRIS product page illustrates department updates with a CSV upload prompt, but this is product-page illustration, not verified help-center instructions for bulk editing. Exact department setup screens, import validation, and inline editing could not be established from the unreadable help center. [Department API guide](https://developer.rippling.com/documentation/rest-api/guides/departments), [HRIS product page, illustration-level evidence](https://www.rippling.com/products/hr/hris)

**Org chart.** Public organizational-data documentation establishes reporting and department data, but a chart-reparenting workflow was not established in the readable sources. Whether the main chart is a direct editor or a view remains **unverified**. Do not treat read-only lookup tools or a marketing chart image as proof either way. [Organizational Data guide](https://developer.rippling.com/documentation/rest-api/guides/org-data-getting-started)

**Access.** The vendor's permissions explanation separates three concepts: **scope** (people whose data are accessible, such as a reporting line or department), **access** (data/app categories), and **actions** (view, edit, approve). Dynamic **Supergroups** assign permission profiles using employee attributes and update as those attributes change. The page states that admins see access gained and lost when a person's role changes. Exact department-descendant matching semantics are not specified there. [Permissions overview](https://www.rippling.com/platform/permissions)

**Exceptions.** The permissions page illustrates an **Include / Except** scope with an IT department included and an office excluded. That is evidence for explicit inclusion/exclusion configuration, not for Sentinel-style membership exceptions propagating through a reporting branch. The same readable page describes Supergroups as the selection mechanism; the separate detailed developer guide currently requires sign-in. [Permissions page and scope illustration](https://www.rippling.com/platform/permissions), [Supergroups guide, login required](https://developer.rippling.com/documentation/platform/supergroups/getting-started)

**First-run guidance.** The public sources establish conceptual configuration guidance, not a first-run department wizard or empty-state copy. The help-center access limit prevents conclusions about those screens. [Permissions conceptual guide](https://www.rippling.com/platform/permissions), [Department guide and linked product help](https://developer.rippling.com/documentation/rest-api/guides/departments)

**Transferable lesson [INFERENCE].** A readable “people covered / permitted services / allowed actions” explanation is more useful here than exposing a general-purpose rule language. Showing access gained/lost is especially relevant when supervisor links also affect permissions.

### 1.5 Workday: supervisory organizations

**Definition.** A supervisory organization groups workers into a management hierarchy, has one superior and potentially many subordinate organizations, and has a Manager role assigned or inherited. Workers belong through jobs or positions. **A manager cannot be a member of the supervisory organization they manage.** Consequently, Workday is a strong analogue for management-hierarchy security, but not an exact match for Sentinel's “head plus everyone below” membership rule. Workday allows organization labels such as business unit, department, group or project. [Superior and Subordinate Organizations](https://doc.workday.com/admin-guide/en-us/manage-workday/organizations/manage-organization-concepts/concept--superior-and-subordinate-organizations.html)

**Setup and maintenance.** **Create Supervisory Organization** is an explicit secured task with name, availability date, subtype, visibility, location and role assignment. Reorganization actions create or change superior/subordinate relationships. For larger changes, **Org Studio and Mass Actions** provides a separate planning/mass-action surface, including organization changes, role assignment and worker moves with a Worksheets hand-off. This is not evidence that a small administrator must start on a freeform chart, nor that a one-column supervisor CSV wizard exists. [Set Up Supervisory Organizations](https://doc.workday.com/admin-guide/en-us/manage-workday/organizations/dan1370797386252.html), [Org Studio and Mass Actions](https://doc.workday.com/admin-guide/en-us/manage-workday/organizations/org-studio-and-mass-actions/prq1551821683080.html)

**Org chart.** Workday documents separate org-view, people-view and hierarchy charts, with domain permissions and exports. Editing/planning is addressed through transaction tasks and Org Studio rather than being synonymous with every organization chart. [Organization Charts](https://doc.workday.com/admin-guide/en-us/manage-workday/organizations/organization-charts/lah1480533274925.html), [Org Studio](https://doc.workday.com/admin-guide/en-us/manage-workday/organizations/org-studio-and-mass-actions/prq1551821683080.html)

**Access.** Role-based constrained security groups tie permissions to organizational roles. Scope choices include **Current Organization Only**, **Current Organization and Unassigned Subordinates**, **Current Organization and All Subordinates**, and **Subordinates to Level**. Role assignments can attach to jobs/positions and inherit down organizations. These are explicit security scopes, not merely chart-display depth. [Workday first-party security course](https://doc.workday.com/workday-education/en-us/course-manuals/security-for-administrators/role-based-security.html), [Role Assignments and Role Inheritance](https://doc.workday.com/admin-guide/en-us/manage-workday/roles/dan1370796642710.html)

**Exceptions.** A lower organization can have a direct role assignee instead of relying on inheritance; different security-group scopes determine where inherited access stops. Organization visibility is a separate configured control. A per-employee membership-override list equivalent to Sentinel's was not established. Workday also warns that rescinding a subordinate-organization creation removes its hierarchy relationship but does not automatically rescind its role assignments, illustrating why structural reversal and access reversal must not be conflated. [Role inheritance](https://doc.workday.com/admin-guide/en-us/manage-workday/roles/dan1370796642710.html), [Setup visibility](https://doc.workday.com/admin-guide/en-us/manage-workday/organizations/dan1370797386252.html), [Rescind behavior](https://doc.workday.com/admin-guide/en-us/manage-workday/organizations/manage-organization-concepts/concept--superior-and-subordinate-organizations.html)

**First-run guidance.** First-party setup documentation specifies prerequisites, required domains and task fields. A non-expert department-setup wizard or empty-state screen was not established. Do not infer that public implementation instructions are the actual first-login experience. [Setup task](https://doc.workday.com/admin-guide/en-us/manage-workday/organizations/dan1370797386252.html)

**Transferable lesson [INFERENCE].** Make inheritance and its boundaries explicit, and show resulting access when structures change. Do not copy Workday's staffing-model, position-role and reorganization-event machinery for this small console.

### 1.6 SAP SuccessFactors Employee Central

**Definition.** Department is an organization-related **Foundation Object**, with a Parent Department field and associations to divisions. It is distinct from employee data, and SAP explicitly says the position structure is independent of the organizational structure. Reporting relationships are employee relationships: one Manager and potentially other relationship types. Thus department hierarchy, position hierarchy and reporting hierarchy are not one interchangeable tree. [Introducing Foundation Objects](https://learning.sap.com/courses/sap-successfactors-employee-central-core-administration/introducing-foundation-objects), [Role-Based Permissions lesson, relationship types](https://learning.sap.com/courses/sap-successfactors-platform-administration/managing-security-using-sap-successfactors-role-based-permissions-rbp_da7298d2-af03-40f1-af0c-e60f9370f80b)

**Setup and maintenance.** SAP documents record management through **Manage Organization, Pay, and Job Structures** for XML-based objects and **Manage Data** for MDF-based objects; their import tools differ. Bulk changes should start with a freshly downloaded template matching the configured object. Employee imports are a separate ordered process and identify managers by `USERID`. This is foundation-data/import administration, not chart-first setup. [Foundation object administration and import](https://learning.sap.com/courses/sap-successfactors-employee-central-core-administration/introducing-foundation-objects), [Importing employees during implementation](https://learning.sap.com/courses/sap-successfactors-employee-central-core-academy/adding-employees-with-the-import-process-during-implementation_eebfa259-d53c-4bc4-9c84-d0df4078c3e1)

**Org chart.** The Company Org Chart is documented as an interactive view of reporting relationships, including matrix relationships, with navigation/search permissions and separately configured card content. The reviewed lesson does not establish drag-to-reparent editing; organization and employee maintenance have separately documented tools. [Using the Org Chart](https://learning.sap.com/courses/sap-successfactors-platform-introduction-academy/using-the-org-chart_fad3d448-ee5f-475a-a499-34f10acf9d26), [Foundation object tools](https://learning.sap.com/courses/sap-successfactors-employee-central-core-administration/introducing-foundation-objects)

**Access.** SAP's explanation is unusually clear: **who** (granted user/group) can do **what** (permission role) **for whom** (target population). Targets can use department/division/location, self, permission groups and manager relationships. Dynamic groups can use employee-field conditions. These are security configurations, not automatic consequences of naming a department leader. [Managing Security Using RBP](https://learning.sap.com/courses/sap-successfactors-platform-administration/managing-security-using-sap-successfactors-role-based-permissions-rbp_da7298d2-af03-40f1-af0c-e60f9370f80b)

**Exceptions.** Dynamic permission-group creation includes people to exclude, and groups can change conditions, inclusions and exclusions. Static permission groups can be imported from CSV and edited through the UI. Users can be in multiple groups, and SAP warns that excessive overlapping groups/roles make consistent access and later changes harder. A denied membership in one group should not be assumed to cancel a separate grant through another role. [RBP group modification, static groups and overlap warnings](https://learning.sap.com/courses/sap-successfactors-platform-administration/managing-security-using-sap-successfactors-role-based-permissions-rbp_da7298d2-af03-40f1-af0c-e60f9370f80b)

**First-run guidance.** SAP explicitly says foundation data must exist before employee data and explains propagation into employee records. The lessons teach configuration and import order; no actual first-run department empty state was observed. [Foundation data prerequisites and propagation](https://learning.sap.com/courses/sap-successfactors-employee-central-core-administration/introducing-foundation-objects)

**Transferable lesson [INFERENCE].** Keep role recipients, action permissions and employee populations conceptually separate, but hide that machinery behind the console's few concrete roles. A custom rule/group builder would be unnecessary here.

### 1.7 Odoo Employees / Departments

Evidence here is pinned to Odoo 18 documentation, not assumed identical across releases.

**Definition.** A department is a record with name, Manager and Parent Department. The employee form also has Department, Manager and Coach fields, with department defaults populating manager/coach. The docs do not say department membership is derived by recursively following each employee's manager; treat the department tree and reporting fields as separate concepts. [Departments](https://www.odoo.com/documentation/18.0/applications/hr/employees/departments.html), [New employee fields](https://www.odoo.com/documentation/18.0/applications/hr/employees/new_employee.html)

**Setup and maintenance.** **Employees → Departments → New** opens a small form. Generic import accepts Excel/CSV, provides templates, and supports updating via import-compatible export/reimport. External IDs support reliable relation matching and repeat imports. The import guide warns that imports are permanent, recommends testing before import, and explains batching. A department-specific inline spreadsheet editor was not established. [Department creation](https://www.odoo.com/documentation/18.0/applications/hr/employees/departments.html), [Export/import guide](https://www.odoo.com/documentation/18.0/applications/essentials/export_import_data.html)

**Org chart.** The same department dashboard offers **Kanban, List and Hierarchy** views. The list shows department, manager, employees and parent. Hierarchy cards show the manager, employee count and child-department count; deeper children start folded. Clicking a card opens the department form, and clicking the employee count opens a list of employees. The documented editor is the form; drag-reparent behavior was not established. [Departments dashboard and hierarchy view](https://www.odoo.com/documentation/18.0/applications/hr/employees/departments.html)

**Access.** Odoo's documentation configures access through user/app rights, groups and record rules. Employee-specific approver fields control different workflows and require corresponding app permissions. Merely selecting the department Manager is not documented as granting access to all descendants' HR records. Do not claim Odoo ships Sentinel's branch-security rule from the department docs alone. [Access rights](https://www.odoo.com/documentation/18.0/applications/general/users/access_rights.html), [Employee approvers](https://www.odoo.com/documentation/18.0/applications/hr/employees/new_employee.html)

**Exceptions.** Per-employee manager, coach and approver fields can differ from department defaults. Broader custom record rules require technical administration; the access-rights guide advises consulting an analyst/support before changing them. A department-level exception register was not established. [Employee configuration](https://www.odoo.com/documentation/18.0/applications/hr/employees/new_employee.html), [Record-rule guidance](https://www.odoo.com/documentation/18.0/applications/general/users/access_rights.html)

**First-run guidance.** The docs give an explicit “New” form path and an import-template path, but no department-specific empty-state screen was observed. The department mini-chart appears after manual save, providing post-entry structural context rather than requiring a chart before entering the form. [Create departments](https://www.odoo.com/documentation/18.0/applications/hr/employees/departments.html), [Import templates](https://www.odoo.com/documentation/18.0/applications/essentials/export_import_data.html)

**Transferable lesson [INFERENCE].** Offer a normal register and a short edit form, with the hierarchy as an alternate view and counts linking to real people. This is more directly reusable than Odoo's broader permission machinery.

### 1.8 Zoho People

**Definition.** Departments are separate records with name/code, a department lead and parent department. Reporting Manager is an employee field. Zoho explicitly says Departments operate independently of its separate Organization Structure feature and that reporting-manager assignment is not restricted by that structure. A lead field is therefore not evidence of automatic branch-derived department membership or access. [Employee Information Operations](https://help.zoho.com/portal/en/kb/people/administrator-guide/operations/employee-information/articles/employee-information-operations), [Organization Structure](https://help.zoho.com/portal/en/kb/people/administrator-guide/settings/manage-accounts/articles/organization-structure-zoho-people)

**Setup and maintenance.** **Settings → Manage Accounts → Users → Import** accepts Excel/CSV with mapping and duplicate handling, including roles, departments and reporting managers. Employee operations provide selected-record **Mass Update** and department import/export. This is documented field/list maintenance, not proof of a drag editor. [Manage Accounts](https://help.zoho.com/portal/en/kb/people/administrator-guide/settings/manage-accounts/articles/manage-accounts-zoho-people), [Employee operations](https://help.zoho.com/portal/en/kb/people/administrator-guide/operations/employee-information/articles/employee-information-operations)

**Org chart.** Zoho's welcome guide describes Organization Tree as a way to understand hierarchy. Team Space separates Reportees, Department and Team List, including direct/indirect-report navigation. A drag-relink chart editor was not established in the sources read; documented employee-field and mass-update paths exist. [Welcome guide](https://www.zoho.com/people/welcome-guide.html), [Team Space](https://help.zoho.com/portal/en/kb/people/administrator-guide/home-tab/articles/team-space)

**Access.** Form record permissions distinguish **My Data / Reportee Data / All Data**. General roles and specific roles are separate; specific roles can apply duties to a location or department, with Enterprise-plan gating. Access to the employee tree is itself a function permission. The department lead's automatic security effect was not established. [Setting up permissions](https://help.zoho.com/portal/en/kb/people/administrator-guide/common-settings/articles/setting-up-permissions-zoho-people), [User Access Control](https://help.zoho.com/portal/en/kb/people/administrator-guide/settings/manage-accounts/articles/user-access-control-zoho-people)

**Exceptions.** Applicability Groups combine conditions such as department/location and individual selection, with a documented restriction to HR-process permission applicability. Specific-role assignment and approval delegation provide other kinds of special treatment. These are not all the same as changing department membership, and approval delegation is not equivalent to granting general HR-data visibility. [User Access Control](https://help.zoho.com/portal/en/kb/people/administrator-guide/settings/manage-accounts/articles/user-access-control-zoho-people), [Employee operations and delegation](https://help.zoho.com/portal/en/kb/people/administrator-guide/operations/employee-information/articles/employee-information-operations)

**First-run guidance.** The Manage Accounts guide organizes setup into organization details, locations, departments, user access control and users/import. “Trigger Onboarding” concerns employee onboarding, not evidence for an organization-setup wizard. No actual department empty-state copy was observed. [Manage Accounts](https://help.zoho.com/portal/en/kb/people/administrator-guide/settings/manage-accounts/articles/manage-accounts-zoho-people)

**Transferable lesson [INFERENCE].** Give routine maintenance a table/filter/mass-update path, and explain manager branch, department membership, delegation and role grants as distinct concepts.

### 1.9 Microsoft Entra ID: dynamic groups and administrative units

**Definition.** Department and Manager are user-profile properties. A dynamic group can select users using rules such as `user.department -eq "Sales"`. An **administrative unit (AU)** is a container used to scope administrative management permissions; AUs cannot be nested, and a user can belong to multiple AUs. Adding a group to an AU brings the group object into scope, not its members' user properties. Importantly, AU scoping does not prevent users from browsing directory information available through their default user permissions. [User profile properties](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/entra/fundamentals/how-to-manage-user-profile-info), [Dynamic membership rules](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/entra/identity/users/groups-dynamic-membership), [Administrative units](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/entra/identity/role-based-access-control/administrative-units)

**Setup and maintenance.** Assigned AU membership can be edited in the admin center or bulk-added using a CSV of user principal names. Dynamic membership instead uses an expression builder/text rule. The rule-validation tool checks sample users/devices, reports membership, and explains individual expressions; it supports checking up to 20 at once. This is explicit setup validation rather than expecting admins to visually inspect a whole chart. [Add AU members](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/entra/identity/role-based-access-control/admin-units-members-add), [Dynamic rules](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/entra/identity/users/groups-dynamic-membership), [Validate rules](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/entra/identity/users/groups-dynamic-rule-validation)

**Org chart.** The user resource exposes Manager and DirectReports relationships, but those relationships are not the AU structure. A native Entra org-chart editing flow was not established. **My Staff** management access is determined by scoped role assignment and its AU, not simply by an HR reporting-line picture. [Graph user resource](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/graph/api/resources/user?view=graph-rest-1.0), [Configure My Staff](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/entra/identity/role-based-access-control/my-staff-configure)

**Access.** An AU scopes supported management roles. A dynamic group can be used by access-control consumers, but membership and resource permission assignment are separate. Entra's special **Direct Reports for manager-ID** rule includes the manager and direct reports only, cannot include their reports recursively, and cannot be combined with other membership rules. It is **not** an implementation of Sentinel's all-level branch scope. Microsoft also warns that access controlled by dynamic attributes is only as secure as the write permissions on those attributes. [AU scope](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/entra/identity/role-based-access-control/administrative-units), [Dynamic rules, direct-report limitations and attribute-write security](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/entra/identity/users/groups-dynamic-membership)

**Exceptions.** Dynamic-group membership cannot be manually added to or removed from. Dynamic AUs likewise give the membership engine sole ownership; add/remove commands are disabled. Changing a dynamic AU to Assigned preserves current members but stops rule processing. This is a clear rule/manual boundary, unlike Sentinel's deliberately separate exception records. [Dynamic group restrictions](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/entra/identity/users/groups-dynamic-membership), [Dynamic AU membership](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/entra/identity/role-based-access-control/admin-units-members-dynamic)

**First-run guidance.** Microsoft describes AU adoption as initial adoption, pruning and stabilization, and supplies member-validation/troubleshooting tools. An empty department screen was not observed. Dynamic-group and dynamic-AU features have license requirements; their capabilities should not be assumed to belong to every Entra installation. [AU adoption](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/entra/identity/role-based-access-control/administrative-units), [Dynamic AU requirements](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/entra/identity/role-based-access-control/admin-units-members-dynamic), [Rule validation](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/entra/identity/users/groups-dynamic-rule-validation)

**Transferable lesson [INFERENCE].** Keep derived membership read-only, express explicit exceptions separately, explain why a person is included, and preview access-affecting changes. Do not copy a generic rule-expression editor or assume an AU is an HR confidentiality boundary.

### 1.10 Google Workspace organizational units

**Definition.** An organizational unit (OU) is a policy/settings container in a hierarchy. A user belongs to exactly one OU, and children inherit settings unless overridden. Manager email and department are separate Directory profile fields; manager email creates the reporting chain shown in Google services. OU placement is not derived automatically from that reporting chain. [How the organizational structure works](https://knowledge.workspace.google.com/admin/users/advanced/how-the-organizational-structure-works), [Directory profile fields](https://knowledge.workspace.google.com/admin/users/add-information-to-a-users-directory-profile)

**Setup and maintenance.** Admins create OUs under **Directory → Organizational units**, choosing the parent. Bulk user creation/update uses CSV with **Org Unit Path**; manager email is also a profile field that can be bulk updated. These are data/container maintenance paths, not drawing reporting lines. [Add an OU](https://knowledge.workspace.google.com/admin/users/advanced/add-an-organizational-unit), [Bulk CSV update](https://knowledge.workspace.google.com/admin/users/add-or-update-multiple-users-from-a-csv-file), [Profile maintenance](https://knowledge.workspace.google.com/admin/users/add-information-to-a-users-directory-profile)

**Org chart.** Google documents displaying a reporting chain from manager email in Google services. The reviewed sources do not establish a dedicated Admin-console reporting-chart editor. Editing the reporting relationship through profile data should not be confused with moving someone between OUs. [Directory reporting-chain fields](https://knowledge.workspace.google.com/admin/users/add-information-to-a-users-directory-profile)

**Access.** OUs control service availability/settings; supported administrative privileges can also be scoped to an OU. Google cautions that only specified privileges support OU restriction, so an arbitrary administrator role is not automatically confined to an OU. Manager email is not documented here as granting employee-record administration. [OU policies](https://knowledge.workspace.google.com/admin/users/advanced/how-the-organizational-structure-works), [Create an admin role for an OU](https://knowledge.workspace.google.com/admin/users/create-an-admin-role-for-an-organizational-unit)

**Exceptions.** Access groups can turn a service **on** for selected users without moving them to another OU, but cannot turn off a service the OU already enables. Configuration groups override service **settings**, a different concept. Google's access-group guide explicitly separates these mechanisms and includes verification by user, service and group. The precedence is documented, not a generic “exceptions always win” rule. [Customize service access using access groups](https://knowledge.workspace.google.com/admin/users/advanced/customize-service-access-using-access-groups)

**First-run guidance.** Everyone initially belongs to the top-level OU. Google says to define an organizational structure only if service access/settings need customization. This is strong guidance against requiring a complex hierarchy just to have one; it is not a reason to remove the department model already approved for Sentinel. [Directory overview](https://knowledge.workspace.google.com/admin/users/overview-set-up-and-manage-the-directory), [Organizational policies FAQ](https://knowledge.workspace.google.com/admin/users/advanced/organizational-policies-faq)

**Transferable lesson [INFERENCE].** Start with the simplest useful structure, explain inherited versus explicit settings, and make exceptions visible without making admins invent a new structural container for every special case. Do not copy Google's permissive service-override precedence into Sentinel's different security model.

### 1.11 ChartHop

**Definition.** A Job has one direct manager and can have a dotted-line manager. Groups such as department, team and location are separate, and a job can belong to multiple groups. Department is one group type, not automatically a manager's branch. [ChartHop terminology](https://docs.charthop.com/charthop-terminology), [Departments](https://docs.charthop.com/departments)

**Setup and maintenance.** ChartHop documents payroll integration or spreadsheet import. The importer can create people/jobs and groups; the Manager column should use the same kind of identifier as the first identifier column, commonly work email. A Data Sheet export can be edited and reimported. The implementation guide orders data import, organization settings/groups, invitations and permissions. [Spreadsheet importing](https://docs.charthop.com/importing-spreadsheet-data), [Import examples](https://docs.charthop.com/spreadsheet-import-examples), [Implementation guide](https://docs.charthop.com/sync-your-org)

**Org chart.** The chart supports search, expansion, zoom and orientation. Unlike a claim that all HR charts are read-only, ChartHop explicitly allows management tasks through **both Org Chart and Data Sheet**. Primary-organization changes take effect immediately; scenario planning is a distinct, plan-dependent way to prepare changes before they go live. [Org Chart](https://docs.charthop.com/org-chart), [About org management](https://docs.charthop.com/about-org-management)

**Access.** Built-in roles determine visible data and permitted actions. The vendor documents filtering sensitive-data roles, for example an Org editor limited to a department/division. The default Employee role includes own personal information and compensation visibility in the reporting line; a separate no-compensation role exists. This is ChartHop's documented policy, not a safe default to copy into an HR/document console. Role filters can be department-limited or custom-filter-limited. [Built-in roles](https://docs.charthop.com/user-roles-and-permissions), [Managing users](https://docs.charthop.com/managing-users)

**Exceptions.** Custom role permissions document Deny overriding Allow. Role/filter assignment provides individual configuration. The User Access Manager's auto-invite filter documents `under:<manager name>` as including skip-level reports, but that example is about invitation targeting; it does **not** establish that every permission-filter surface accepts that same transitive query. [Custom role precedence](https://docs.charthop.com/VUlQVowhVgys4dPjfhzid), [User Access Manager](https://docs.charthop.com/user-access-manager), [Managing users](https://docs.charthop.com/managing-users)

**First-run guidance.** ChartHop says the org chart must be set up before configuring user access, and that people in the chart still need access granted. Its implementation guidance calls out duplicate data, inconsistent grouping and role misconfiguration. No actual empty-state copy was observed. This distinguishes “employee is represented” from “employee has a login,” a critical distinction for Sentinel's many employees and few accounts. [User Access Manager](https://docs.charthop.com/user-access-manager), [Implementation guide](https://docs.charthop.com/sync-your-org)

**Transferable lesson [INFERENCE].** Keep list and chart views of the same data, and separate working employee records from account grants. Do not introduce scenario planning, effective-dated reorganization tooling or a query language just because the chart-focused product has them.

## 3. UX guidance applicable to the redesign

### 3.1 Deep hierarchy versus table or drill-down

PatternFly recommends tree views when users need to communicate/select a hierarchy, but specifically says not to use one for an overview of a large amount of data or a hierarchy that is too deep and contains hundreds of items; it suggests drill-down for the latter. It distinguishes tree tables (nested tabular information) from tree navigation (selection changes the content). This does not forbid an org-chart view; it argues against making that view the only way to complete setup. [PatternFly tree-view design guidance](https://www.patternfly.org/components/tree-view/design-guidelines/)

Carbon documents tables for finding a specific resource, sorting/searching/filtering, row actions and expandable supplementary detail. It also warns against treating its data table as a full spreadsheet application. **Application [INFERENCE]:** a department register with head, employee count and setup status can be a normal table; repairing many employee supervisor fields can reuse existing import/bulk tools rather than requiring a new spreadsheet engine. [Carbon data-table guidelines](https://www.carbondesignsystem.com/building-blocks/core/components/data-table/guidelines)

A real tree widget also carries keyboard and screen-reader obligations: distinguish focus from selection; provide expand/collapse states and appropriate navigation; expose levels/positions when nodes are dynamically loaded. Adding `role="tree"` to a canvas does not by itself meet those requirements. **Application [INFERENCE]:** use a straightforward list/drill-down where it solves the task; retain a dedicated, accessible hierarchy only where relationships are the task. [WAI-ARIA Tree View pattern](https://www.w3.org/WAI/ARIA/apg/patterns/treeview/)

### 3.2 Bulk actions without accidental bulk consequences

Carbon's batch-action pattern activates after selection, shows actions applying to the selected set, and disables competing per-row action menus while batch mode is active. It provides explicit cancellation. **Application [INFERENCE]:** show selection counts and distinguish “selected rows” from “all matching results”; use batch actions only where the same operation legitimately applies to all selected records, such as an existing supervisor correction/import or service configuration. Do not build mass employee-to-department assignment that contradicts Sentinel's derived-membership model. [Carbon batch actions](https://www.carbondesignsystem.com/building-blocks/core/components/data-table/guidelines#batch-actions), [local derived model](department-orgtree-rework.md#derivation-backendappservicesdepartment_tree_servicepy-new)

Bulk safety is not merely visual. Odoo and BambooHR explicitly warn about irreversible imports, while Entra provides rule validation against sample people. **Application [INFERENCE]:** reuse templates with stable identifiers and show the people affected before access-changing operations; do not assume a successful file upload or a valid tree proves correct authorization. [Odoo import safety](https://www.odoo.com/documentation/18.0/applications/essentials/export_import_data.html), [BambooHR import training](https://partners.bamboohr.com/reseller-training-resources/import-tool-training), [Entra validation](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/entra/identity/users/groups-dynamic-rule-validation)

### 3.3 Progressive disclosure, not hidden essentials

NN/g recommends showing the important common options first and placing advanced/rare options on a clearly labeled secondary surface. The split must match actual task frequency and the path to advanced controls must be obvious. NN/g cautions that more than two disclosure levels often causes disorientation. **Application [INFERENCE]:** make “Create department” and “Edit department” visible labels, not a building icon buried in a menu on every employee. Keep deputies, viewers and exceptions in the selected department's clearly labeled detail area. Services can be configured there too, but **must remain visibly incomplete until configured** if needed for activation; “advanced” must not hide a required prerequisite. [Progressive Disclosure](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/progressive-disclosure/), [local services and preflight contract](department-orgtree-rework.md#api-contract-apiv1departments-gated-by-departmentsmanage-unless-noted)

NN/g's complex-application guidance recommends reducing clutter without removing capabilities, making critical information salient, and showing results while users act. **Application [INFERENCE]:** preview the selected manager's coverage alongside the department form, rather than showing the whole organization, a rail, a chart toolbar, global switch and every administrative control simultaneously. [8 Design Guidelines for Complex Applications](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/complex-application-design/)

### 3.4 Wizards versus resumable setup tasks

NN/g recommends wizards for novices and infrequent configuration, with named steps, visible progress, back navigation, self-contained steps and saved progress. It also warns that repeated wizards are tedious, make cross-step comparisons difficult, and can obstruct needed information. **Application [INFERENCE]:** a short first-department flow may help; forcing the same global wizard for every rename, deputy change or supervisor correction would not. [Wizards: Definition and Design Recommendations](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/wizards/)

GOV.UK reserves its multi-task pattern for longer transactions completed over multiple sessions, advises simplifying first, groups related work into tasks, and uses explicit completion states. Where possible it lets users complete tasks in any order. **Application [INFERENCE]:** start with a compact setup checklist derived from real persisted data, not a separate onboarding engine. Use a linear wizard only for genuinely ordered steps; let independent department setup happen in any order. [Complete multiple tasks](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/patterns/complete-multiple-tasks/)

### 3.5 Empty states and in-context learning

NN/g says empty states should explain system status, teach what belongs there and provide a direct action. Loading, no data and no matching results must not share a misleading “No records” message. Its onboarding research favors dismissible, recallable contextual help over intrusive tutorials shown before the task. **Application [INFERENCE]:** “No departments yet. Choose a manager; their reporting branch becomes the department” with **Create department** is more actionable than an empty canvas or a tutorial carousel. “No results for this search” needs a clear-filter action, not the same first-run message. [Empty states in complex applications](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/empty-state-interface-design/), [Onboarding Tutorials vs. Contextual Help](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/onboarding-tutorials/)

### 3.6 High-impact edits and preflight

WCAG's error-prevention guidance calls for important submissions to be reversible, checked or reviewable/confirmable; its intent is not a confirmation dialog for every ordinary save. Rippling also advertises showing access gained/lost when roles change, and Entra warns that dynamic security depends on write permissions for source attributes. **Application [INFERENCE]:** preview a supervisor move or head change that affects many people, and use a deliberate review screen for turning on scoping. Do not put a generic “Are you sure?” on every label edit, and do not silently change the already-approved permission contract for editing supervisor links. [WCAG 3.3.4 understanding](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/error-prevention-legal-financial-data.html), [Rippling access changes](https://www.rippling.com/platform/permissions), [Entra attribute security](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/entra/identity/users/groups-dynamic-membership), [approved editor permission](department-orgtree-rework.md#user-decisions-verbatim-intent)

### 3.7 Mobile and Arabic/English limits

NN/g's mobile-table guidance requires legible columns without zoom, recommends sticky context and selecting a relevant subset of data, and treats forced rotation as a last resort. It notes that wordy data may fit only two columns on a narrow screen. **Application [INFERENCE]:** use a department list followed by a full-screen detail/branch drill-down on phones, not a desktop table, rail, canvas and inspector squeezed together. Keep single-department editing usable; make bulk comparison/reorganization desktop-oriented without removing the safe non-drag route on mobile. [Mobile Tables](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/mobile-tables/)

WCAG 2.5.7 requires a single-pointer alternative to authored drag functionality; keyboard support alone does not satisfy that pointer requirement. **Application [INFERENCE]:** “Change supervisor” with a searchable picker is a first-class action, not merely a fallback to dragging lines. If custom pan/zoom remains, it also needs appropriate non-drag operation. [Dragging Movements](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/dragging-movements.html)

W3C recommends semantic `dir` for RTL, logical start/end layout properties, and appropriate direction handling for user-entered mixed-script text. **Application [INFERENCE]:** employee search, Arabic/English names, hierarchy indentation and detail navigation must work in both directions; direction is not just mirroring a screenshot. This also follows the local product's equal-language and keyboard-accessibility contract. [W3C structural RTL guidance](https://www.w3.org/International/questions/qa-html-dir), [local accessibility contract](../../PRODUCT.md#accessibility--inclusion)

### 4.2 Candidate UX approaches and tradeoffs

All three approaches retain the approved manager-anchored model, deputies/viewers, exceptions, services, preflight and global enforcement switch. They are alternate presentations, not proposals to replace the data model. [Approved requirements](department-orgtree-rework.md#user-decisions-verbatim-intent)

#### A. Department register + focused detail + compact setup checklist (recommended)

**Flow [INFERENCE]:** open Departments → see existing departments or the explanatory empty state → Create department → choose head and review coverage → configure names, access and services → return to the register. For first-time setup, show a compact checklist with links to unfinished work and the final enforcement review. In maintenance, open the same department detail directly. Reporting lines and organization chart are named secondary views, not competing permanent columns.

**Strengths [INFERENCE]:** fits short repeat visits; supports direct lookup and the small number of login accounts; exposes completion without requiring chart-navigation skills; can reuse existing forms, lists and APIs. It borrows [Odoo's list/form alternative](https://www.odoo.com/documentation/18.0/applications/hr/employees/departments.html), [Personio's maintenance paths](https://support.personio.de/hc/en-us/articles/115001324149-Overview-of-the-People-List), and [progressive disclosure](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/progressive-disclosure/).

**Costs and limits [INFERENCE]:** relationships are less immediately visible than a chart. Counter with “parent / reporting context,” expandable sub-departments and a View branch link, not a permanently visible second tree. A large checklist would be unnecessary ceremony; keep it compact and remove its dominance after setup. [PatternFly tree-selection guidance](https://www.patternfly.org/components/tree-view/design-guidelines/), [GOV.UK: simplify before adding task lists](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/patterns/complete-multiple-tasks/)

#### B. Unit-first guided setup, then the same register for maintenance

**Flow [INFERENCE]:** choose one of the eight duty units → inspect its existing root and reporting branch → choose the manager(s) that should define departments → name and configure each → review that unit's result → continue to another unit → organization-wide enforcement review. Persist real department configuration as it is saved; allow returning without replaying all earlier screens. The everyday entry remains the register from A.

**Strengths [INFERENCE]:** reduces the initial problem to one familiar unit and makes the administrative-office split concrete. Good when the admin wants “walk me through setup.” This follows [NN/g's novice/infrequent setup guidance](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/wizards/) and [contextual learning guidance](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/onboarding-tutorials/).

**Costs and limits [INFERENCE]:** more navigation; can incorrectly imply every unit must be a department or that setup must happen in one rigid sequence. Do not auto-create eight departments, reset existing work, or require the guided route for routine edits. If the steps depend heavily on cross-unit comparison, A is better; [NN/g explicitly warns about cross-step comparison and repeated wizard cost](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/wizards/).

#### C. Branch navigator + inspector, with an optional chart view

**Flow [INFERENCE]:** select a duty unit → search or drill down through a compact branch navigator → select a manager → use one stable inspector to Create department or edit an existing boundary. Offer the full chart as an alternate view of the selected branch, with employee-list reporting corrections alongside a dedicated reporting task, not inside every card's department menu.

**Strengths [INFERENCE]:** makes the manager-anchored model tangible and helps admins who already think in reporting chains; preserves more of the current tree-oriented interface. A navigator plus a stable detail surface is consistent with [PatternFly's primary-detail tree usage](https://www.patternfly.org/components/tree-view/design-guidelines/).

**Costs and limits [INFERENCE]:** still requires understanding a deep hierarchy and has more keyboard/mobile implementation burden. Restrict the navigator to the current branch, with search, breadcrumbs and counts; do not expand all 174 supervisors or all 304 people. [PatternFly warns against deep trees of hundreds](https://www.patternfly.org/components/tree-view/design-guidelines/), [WAI-ARIA defines nontrivial tree interaction requirements](https://www.w3.org/WAI/ARIA/apg/patterns/treeview/), and [NN/g documents small-screen data limits](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/mobile-tables/).

### 4.3 Recommended choice

**Choose A as the default, with a small amount of B's contextual unit guidance and C's branch preview only when choosing or checking a head [INFERENCE].** The key shift is from “edit an organization diagram and discover its controls” to “create a named department, check its people, and review who can use it.” Keep reporting corrections explicit and chart inspection available, without constructing a second membership model, a general permission-rule builder or a full reorganization-planning system. This preserves the [approved local model](department-orgtree-rework.md#user-decisions-verbatim-intent) while applying [list/form precedents](https://www.odoo.com/documentation/18.0/applications/hr/employees/departments.html), [progressive disclosure](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/progressive-disclosure/), and [contextual setup guidance](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/empty-state-interface-design/).
